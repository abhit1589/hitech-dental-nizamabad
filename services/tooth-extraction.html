What is Tooth Extraction & Surgery?

Tooth extraction is the removal of a tooth from its socket in the jawbone. While we always try to save natural teeth, extraction becomes necessary when a tooth is severely damaged, infected, or causing problems. Our oral surgeon performs simple extractions and complex surgical extractions, including wisdom teeth removal, with minimal discomfort and fast recovery.

Who Needs This Service?

  • Severely decayed tooth that cannot be saved
  • Advanced gum disease loosening teeth
  • Impacted wisdom teeth causing pain or infection
  • Crowded teeth requiring orthodontic space
  • Broken teeth beyond repair
  • Infection or abscess not responding to root canal

Aftercare Tips

  • Bite on gauze for 30-45 minutes to stop bleeding
  • Apply ice packs for 15 minutes on, 15 minutes off to reduce swelling
  • Take prescribed pain medication as directed
  • Eat soft foods for 24-48 hours (yogurt, soup, mashed potatoes)
  • Avoid smoking, using straws, or spitting forcefully (causes dry socket)
  • Rinse gently with warm salt water after 24 hours
  • Rest and avoid strenuous activity for 48 hours

Frequently Asked Questions

Is tooth extraction painful?

No. Local anesthesia ensures you feel no pain during the procedure. You may feel pressure but not pain. After the numbness wears off, mild discomfort is normal and manageable with over-the-counter or prescribed pain relievers.

How long does recovery take?

Most patients heal within 7-10 days. Swelling and discomfort peak at 2-3 days and then gradually improve. Full bone healing takes 3-4 months, but you'll feel normal much sooner.

What is dry socket?

Dry socket occurs when the blood clot dislodges prematurely, exposing bone and nerves. It causes severe pain 3-4 days after extraction. Avoid smoking, using straws, and vigorous rinsing to prevent it. If it occurs, we can treat it quickly.

When should wisdom teeth be removed?

Wisdom teeth should be removed if they're impacted, causing pain, damaging adjacent teeth, or prone to infection. Many dentists recommend removal in late teens or early twenties before roots fully form, making extraction easier.

Can I drive home after extraction?

Yes, if you had only local anesthesia. If you received sedation, arrange for someone to drive you home and stay with you for a few hours.

What are my tooth replacement options?

After extraction, you can replace the tooth with a dental implant, bridge, or partial denture. We'll discuss the best option for your needs during your consultation.
