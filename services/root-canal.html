What is Root Canal Treatment?

Root Canal Treatment (RCT), also known as endodontic therapy, is a dental procedure designed to save a tooth that has become infected or badly damaged. The treatment involves removing the infected or inflamed pulp (soft tissue inside the tooth), cleaning and disinfecting the root canals, and then sealing them to prevent future infection.

At Hitech Dental Clinic, our M.D.S endodontist Dr. Nanda Kumar Kommineni specializes in root canal procedures using modern rotary endodontic techniques, ensuring a comfortable, efficient, and successful treatment experience.

Who Needs Root Canal Treatment?

You may need a root canal if you experience any of these symptoms:

  • Severe toothache — persistent or throbbing pain that worsens when chewing or applying pressure
  • Prolonged sensitivity — lingering pain when consuming hot or cold foods and drinks
  • Tooth discoloration — darkening or graying of the tooth
  • Swollen, tender gums — swelling near the affected tooth, sometimes with a pimple-like bump
  • Abscess or infection — visible signs of infection confirmed by X-ray
  • Deep decay or cavity — when decay reaches the tooth's nerve
  • Cracked or broken tooth — exposing the pulp to bacteria

Early intervention can save your natural tooth and prevent the need for extraction.

What to Expect: Root Canal Procedure Steps

1

Examination & X-Ray

We examine the tooth and take digital X-rays to assess the extent of infection and plan treatment.

2

Local Anesthesia

The area around the tooth is numbed with local anesthesia to ensure a painless procedure. You'll remain awake but won't feel discomfort.

3

Pulp Removal

We create a small opening in the tooth crown and carefully remove the infected or damaged pulp tissue using specialized instruments.

4

Cleaning & Shaping

Root canals are thoroughly cleaned, disinfected, and shaped using rotary endodontic technology for precision and efficiency.

5

Sealing the Canals

The cleaned canals are filled with a biocompatible rubber-like material (gutta-percha) and sealed to prevent reinfection.

6

Temporary Filling

A temporary filling is placed to close the opening until your next visit for permanent restoration.

7

Crown Placement

In a follow-up appointment (usually within 7-10 days), a permanent crown or filling is placed to restore the tooth's full strength and function.

Aftercare Tips

Following root canal treatment, proper care ensures successful healing and longevity of your tooth:

  • Avoid chewing on the treated tooth until the permanent crown is placed
  • Maintain oral hygiene — brush twice daily and floss gently around the treated area
  • Take prescribed medications — complete any antibiotics or pain relievers as directed
  • Attend follow-up appointments — don't skip your crown placement visit
  • Watch for warning signs — contact us if you experience severe pain, swelling, or an allergic reaction
  • Avoid hard foods for a few days after treatment

With proper care and a crown restoration, a root canal-treated tooth can last a lifetime.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is root canal treatment painful?

No. Modern root canal treatment is virtually painless thanks to effective local anesthesia and advanced techniques. Most patients report that the procedure feels similar to getting a routine filling. The treatment actually relieves the severe pain caused by the infection.

How long does a root canal take?

A typical root canal appointment takes 60-90 minutes, depending on the tooth (front teeth are quicker than molars with multiple roots). Most cases are completed in a single visit, though complex infections may require a second appointment.

What is the success rate of root canal treatment?

Root canal treatment has a success rate of over 95% when performed by a specialist and followed by proper restoration. With good oral hygiene and regular dental checkups, a root canal-treated tooth can last a lifetime.

Can I drive home after the procedure?

Yes. Root canal treatment uses only local anesthesia, so you'll be fully alert and able to drive immediately after your appointment. The numbness typically wears off within 2-3 hours.

Is it better to extract the tooth or get a root canal?

Saving your natural tooth with a root canal is almost always the better option. Natural teeth preserve jawbone structure, maintain proper bite alignment, and avoid the need for bridges or implants. Extraction should only be considered when a tooth cannot be saved.
