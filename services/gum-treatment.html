Gum Treatment

Restore healthy gums and prevent tooth loss from gum disease

What is Gum Treatment?

Gum treatment (periodontal therapy) addresses gum disease caused by bacterial infection of the tissues supporting your teeth. Treatment ranges from deep cleaning (scaling and root planing) to surgical procedures, depending on disease severity. Early treatment can reverse gingivitis and manage periodontitis, preventing tooth loss and protecting your overall health.

Who Needs This Service?

  • Red, swollen, or bleeding gums
  • Gums that have pulled away from teeth (receding gums)
  • Persistent bad breath or bad taste
  • Loose or shifting teeth
  • Pus between teeth and gums
  • Changes in bite or fit of dentures
  • Diagnosis of gingivitis or periodontitis

What to Expect

1

Comprehensive Gum Evaluation

We measure pocket depths, check for bleeding, and take X-rays to assess bone loss.

2

Deep Cleaning (Scaling & Root Planing)

Remove plaque and tartar from below the gum line and smooth root surfaces.

3

Antimicrobial Therapy

Antibiotic gel or rinse may be placed in pockets to kill remaining bacteria.

4

Follow-Up & Maintenance

Regular periodontal maintenance cleanings every 3-4 months.

5

Surgical Treatment (If Needed)

Flap surgery or bone grafts for advanced cases to regenerate lost tissue.

6

Ongoing Monitoring

Long-term management to keep gum disease under control.

Aftercare Tips

  • Brush twice daily with soft-bristled toothbrush
  • Floss daily or use interdental brushes
  • Use antimicrobial mouthwash as prescribed
  • Attend maintenance cleanings every 3-4 months
  • Avoid smoking (significantly worsens gum disease)
  • Maintain good nutrition and manage systemic conditions like diabetes

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes gum disease?

Gum disease is caused by plaque buildup (bacterial film) on teeth. Without proper brushing and flossing, plaque hardens into tartar, causing inflammation. Risk factors include smoking, genetics, diabetes, hormonal changes, and certain medications.

Is gum disease reversible?

Gingivitis (early gum disease) is completely reversible with professional cleaning and improved home care. Periodontitis (advanced gum disease) cannot be fully reversed but can be managed to prevent further damage.

Is gum treatment painful?

Deep cleaning may cause mild discomfort, so local anesthesia is typically used. Most patients report little to no pain during or after treatment. Any tenderness resolves within a few days.

How long does gum treatment take?

Scaling and root planing usually requires 2-4 visits, treating one or two sections of the mouth per visit. Each session takes 45-90 minutes. Surgical treatments, if needed, are scheduled separately.

Will my gums grow back after treatment?

Gum tissue does not regenerate significantly once lost. However, treatment stops further recession and can sometimes allow minor reattachment. Gum grafts can restore lost tissue if needed for aesthetics or sensitivity.

Can gum disease affect my overall health?

Yes. Research links gum disease to increased risk of heart disease, stroke, diabetes complications, respiratory infections, and pregnancy complications. Treating gum disease improves both oral and overall health.
